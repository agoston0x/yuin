What is different

The usual way

  • A provider holds the mapping from an email to an address.
  • That provider goes down and your users are locked out.
  • Auth rules live in your code, so changing one is a deploy.
  • Leaving means asking for an export.

Here

  • The mapping is a salted hash on chain. Nobody holds the other half.
  • Any node can sign a user in; a majority has to agree.
  • Auth rules are records. Tick a box and the next call obeys it.
  • Add your own wallet as an owner and walk out with the account.

How a sign-in goes

  1. A key is made in the browser — before any provider is contacted, and it never leaves.
  2. The provider signs a token that names that key, and nothing else can use it.
  3. The nodes check it — the signature, the audience, the key — and a majority sign an attestation.
  4. An account appears, with a session key that expires and cannot spend past its cap.

Lose the device and a recovery goes through a quorum, then waits in the open where you can stop it. A recovery nobody objects to is a recovery. One you are awake for is a theft.

What we are not claiming

  • Nodes see the provider's token while they verify it. Privacy here is optimistic, not cryptographic.
  • A targeted attacker who already knows your provider subject can confirm a guess against the registry.
  • The node set is small and known today. The registry and the slashing are what make it open later.
